# Level design audit and variety ideas

A review of how the campaign routes actually play, what was changed in response, and a backlog of ideas for making chapters more varied without simply making them harder.

## What the routes were doing

Reading `levels.ts` and the generated output together, every chapter was built from one recipe: a strictly linear chain of main shelves, each one only reachable from the one before it. Concretely:

1. **One path, always.** The only alternatives to the main chain were three optional signal shelves per region and a salvage floor under two specific gaps. On 43 of 45 shelves in a region there was exactly one place to be.
2. **Falling was binary.** Every gap that was not one of the two salvage gaps was a death pit. Deaths cost the whole stretch since the last checkpoint, so the sensible strategy was to never attempt anything but the safe arc. Nothing in the route rewarded a bold jump.
3. **Every gap was the same jump.** Required gaps sat in a 90–350 unit band and rises under ~230 units, all cleared with the same held-jump arc. There was no jump that asked for a different technique (a low fast hop, a full-hold leap, a drop-and-brake).
4. **Every encounter faced the same way.** All spawns sat 250–620 units ahead of the landing, facing Hopper. Combat was always "walk right, deal with what is in front". Nothing came from behind, nothing from above except the four flyer species' dives.
5. **Blocking was a separate verb with no relationship to the kick.** The shield was a hold, cost a resource, and suspended the two attacks. It made defence passive: you stopped moving, absorbed, and then resumed. Nothing about it made a fight _more_ interesting to win.
6. **Hits were free.** Knockback was 230 units/s and steering re-acquired control within a tenth of a second, so being hit cost one armor pip and nothing else. Edges were never dangerous.
7. **Landing tolerance was strict.** A jump that fell three pixels short slid down the face into the pit. Combined with (2) this taught the player to under-commit.

Everything the player did in a chapter was therefore: run right, one held jump per gap, fight what is ahead, repeat. The nine-shelf rhythm of a chapter was correct, but each of the nine shelves asked for the same input.

Verified after the change: 405/405 actual-engine route transitions, 54 high roads, 60 catch floors and 27 signal shelves all reachable, no failures in the ballistic audit, and full-hold-at-speed jumps at 5–8 per mission (2–5 before the chapter signatures, 1–3 before the longer crossings, 7–9 before relief was damped).

## Ideas for more varied movement

These are ordered by how much of the existing engine they reuse. Everything except item 7 (stomp chains, declined) is now implemented; see **Implemented since** below.

1. **Give each chapter a movement signature.** The generator already knows the chapter's beat (`learn`, `climb`, `drop`, `vista`…) and does nothing with it beyond spawn placement. Let the beat pick a gap style: `climb` chapters use short gaps with tall rises (tap-jump ladders), `drop` chapters use long horizontal gaps from height (hold, then release to brake), `vista` a single huge crossing, `run` a run of low fast hops that punish a full hold. The player would then change technique every chapter rather than every mission.
2. **Low road under the fight.** Mirror the high road: a solid underpass beneath some fight shelves, entered by dropping off the previous landing's far edge, exiting up a two-step stair. The renderer's architectural fill would need a hollow, but it gives a stealth route and a place to put the signal shelves that is not always "up".
3. **Bounce shelves.** A `kind: 'spring'` platform that launches at 1.3× jump speed on landing. Used sparingly it makes a vista crossing possible without a hold, and a spring under a catch floor is a joyful way back up instead of the current step.
4. **Moving main-route shelves.** `moving` is currently reserved for optional shelves. One moving shelf per region on the main route (industrial cranes, drifting reef) forces the player to time a jump rather than just aim it. The audit script already simulates moving platforms at phase 0; extend it to test the worst phase.
5. **Wind lanes and conveyors as real routes.** `wind` hazards and `conveyor` shelves exist but are used once. A conveyor running _against_ Hopper on a fight shelf makes the parry-stagger window the only way to gain ground; a crosswind over a crossing turns the catch floor from a safety net into the intended path.
6. **Wall kick.** Hopper stops dead on a solid face. Let a jump pressed within 0.1 s of touching a wall push off it at 60% speed. With the ledge catch it makes tall faces climbable in two moves, and it lets `climb` chapters use vertical shafts instead of stairs.
7. **Stomp chains as traversal.** Stomping already rebounds (520/820 units/s). Place two or three flyers over a gap that is otherwise too long: the intended crossing is a chain of stomps. The parry makes it safe to attempt because their dive can be met head-on if the chain breaks.
8. **Gravity gates on the main route in the third episode.** The inversion gallery is optional and appears once. Violet Inversion could put one short mandatory inverted stretch in its `drop` chapter, with the ceiling shelves doing the work of the high road. The engine and audit already handle it.
9. **Ambush variety by species.** Rear ambushes currently emerge in place. Burrowers could surface _under_ Hopper (forcing a jump), thorn choirs could form a firing line on the high road that the ground route walks beneath, and mirror stalkers could mirror Hopper's jumps on the parallel shelf.
10. **Crossing pairs.** Instead of one long crossing, split some into two medium gaps with a small island whose only purpose is to be a bad place to be: a rear ambusher emerges on it the moment Hopper lands, so the correct play is to jump straight through without stopping.
11. **Let the signal shelves teach.** The three optional shelves per region are placed by index. Tie each to one of the new verbs: one only reachable by a ledge catch, one by parrying a shot to break a barrier, one by the high road. The collectible then rewards learning the mechanic rather than spotting the shelf.

Item 7 was declined and stays unimplemented.

## Implemented since

- **Chapter movement signatures (idea 1).** `SIGNATURES` in `levels.ts` gives each of a region's five chapters a gap multiplier and an elevation profile: how the chapter's fixed elevation change is spread over its nine shelves. Lesson is even; ladder (chapter 2) uses 0.72× gaps and takes the climb in 1.4-share chunks between flat 0.4-share landings; glide (chapter 3) uses 1.22× gaps front-loaded from height; sprint (chapter 4) uses 0.66× gaps with near-flat shelves and two tall steps; summit (chapter 5) mixes them. Chapter names and elevation targets are unchanged. Full-hold-at-speed jumps rose to 5–8 per mission, deliberately: the ladder chapters are where they live. Every route transition still passes the real engine.
- **Wall kick (idea 6).** Touching a solid face records a 0.12 s grace; a jump pressed inside it launches away from the wall at 92% jump speed and 78% run speed with steering committed for 0.16 s. It counts as a takeoff, so the rear launch strike applies.
- **Low road (idea 2).** The glide chapter's fight shelf is hollowed (`hollow: 200`, the renderer stops its architectural fill there) and a corridor floor runs from under the previous landing's far edge to under the following gap, 360 units below the higher of the two shelves. Drop off the previous edge with the stick pulled back (pressing forward lets the ledge catch grab the fight shelf instead), walk beneath the encounter, and climb two one-way steps in the far gap back onto the route. The audit checks the floor sits under the previous edge, the corridor clears 200 units under the shelf's collision, and floor → step → step → next landing are all reachable. 3 per mission.
- **Spring pads (idea 3).** `kind: 'spring'` launches at 1.4× jump speed (the doc said 1.3×; 1.4× was needed for the catch floor to clear the tallest landing rise, and holding A still adds float). One sits on every crossing catch floor as the way back up, and one at the takeoff of every glide crossing so the long leap needs no hold. Chevrons breathe on the pad so it reads as a launcher.
- **Swinging main shelf (idea 4).** The summit chapter's fourth shelf moves ±90 units at 60 units/s. Both audits test it at four quarter phases and require the worst to pass; the engine test starts each attempt at the shifted takeoff point.
- **Belts and wind as routes (idea 5).** The summit chapter's second fight runs on a conveyor with `drift: -70` (chevrons run backward): stand still and it carries Hopper toward the edge behind, so ground is only gained by pressing through the parry-stagger windows. A wind lane over every glide crossing pushes −150 across and +400 down (gravity +21%) on an airborne Hopper; the spring at the takeoff still clears it, an ordinary held jump usually does not, and the catch floor below is the honest line.
- **Required inverted crossing (idea 8).** Violet Inversion's glide chapter widens its seventh gap to 2.6× (1,092 units, verified unjumpable) and spans it with a gate 820 units up whose ceiling shelf runs 260 units past both landings. Leap up into it from the shelf's end, walk the ceiling across, and drop out over the next shelf (`via: 'inversion'`). Falling out mid-way lands on a catch floor with a spring that puts Hopper straight back into the gate. The route test scripts the whole traversal.
- **Ambush variety by species (idea 9).** A basalt burrower tagged for a rear ambush becomes `under`: buried where Hopper will walk, the ground gives a tell for 0.45 s once Hopper is within 210 units, then it surfaces beneath whoever is still standing there. A mirror stalker with a high road available becomes `mirror`: it stays on the high shelf, tracks Hopper's x at 480 units/s, and pounces straight down when Hopper walks beneath, springing back up afterward. A thorn choir overhead ambush spawns a second choir on the second high shelf so the ground route walks under a firing line.
- **Crossing islands (idea 10).** Summit crossings are split into two 0.55-length gaps around a 240-unit island. A shadow lies dormant on it with `wake: 40`, so it emerges behind Hopper almost as soon as Hopper lands; the clean line is straight through. The audit requires the island to be no wider than 260 and its lurker present.
- **Signal shelves teach (idea 11).** Chapter 2's signal lip sits at `400·g^-0.75 + 45` units, just past a full jump's apex, so only the ledge catch reaches it (the audit verifies it is unreachable by a clean landing but reachable to a lip 80 lower). Chapter 3's signal is inside a hexagonal cage with the region's ranged type standing in it as warden; enemy shots pass out, lasers stop at the bars, and only a parried shot flying back in opens it. Chapter 5's signal moved from a run shelf to 235 units above the second high-road shelf and is verified unreachable from the ground.
- **Combat pass.** After the movement work, `COMBAT_AUDIT.md` covers agile techniques, waves, crossing guards, the challenge index and the boss enclosure.
- **Right-stick look.** Not from the list, but it changes how routes read: the stick drifts the rendered camera up to 520 units ahead or behind and 340 up or down while widening the view, eased both ways, layered over the tracking camera. Scouting a crossing, a high road or a catch floor before committing is now possible without moving.
