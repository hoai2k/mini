How the Play Goes

A toy theatre in five acts. Each act is staged with a different piece of stage machinery; its card tells you what the A button does. Keepsakes you carry from act to act decide how the story ends.

  • Three lanterns are your hull or heart in every act. A hit costs one, then you have a moment’s grace.
  • Lose them all and you carry on from the last checkpoint, such as the last key item you picked up or the last stretch of the stage you reached, with whatever you had gathered by then. Restart Act in the pause menu goes back to the top.
  • Scene Select (pause menu, or the film-strip button on the title) starts any scene you have reached. Acts you have reached can also be replayed from the playbill.

Act I — The Crossing

Sail the painted sea to the enchanted tower before the sea witch wrecks you. The stagehands crank three rows of waves; hop between them.

  • Rocks rise through trapdoors where the water churns. Leap over them or change rows.
  • Lightning falls on whichever row the witch’s cloud hangs above. Leaping won’t help — change rows.
  • The wind shifts. Watch the vane: a headwind pushes you back and slows the voyage, a tailwind carries you on.
  • Pearls score, and mend the hull: every ten pearls restore a lantern (a part-mended lantern glows from the bottom up). Three tea leaves float by once each; together they complete the reading, which warns you of danger in the acts to come.
  • When the bay is in sight the deck becomes a party: press A on the beat to light the lanterns.
  • Three lanterns are your hull. Lose them all and the sea keeps you.

Act II — The Sleepers’ Grotto

The ship is gone. Swim down through the witch’s grotto after the sinking guitar, waking the drowned revellers from the party as you go, and catch the guitar on the cave floor.

  • Swim in any direction on your wires; you drift slowly down on your own. A kicks: a burst of speed, then a moment to recover.
  • The guitar sinks just ahead of you and sets the pace; it can’t be caught until it settles on the cave floor.
  • Air runs down as you swim. Hover in the bubbles over an air vent to fill up. Run out and you lose a lantern.
  • Wake the sleepers. Swim into a conch horn to sound it (each sounds once, then turns grey); while it sings, any sleeper you pass wakes and floats up. Once you hold the guitar, its music wakes them too. Everyone you wake joins the pageant at the end.
  • Clams open for one beat on their own rhythm: swim in while one is open for its pearl. After you take it the clam rests shut for a while, then regrows one more pearl, which you can watch grow each time it opens; take it once it’s full size. After that the clam stays shut and turns grey. A closed clam is solid.
  • Hazards: eels lunge from holes whose eyes glow first; jellyfish drift and sting; the red dragon patrols the deep cave. Each costs a lantern. With the full tea reading, an omen glints a beat before each strikes.
  • The witch’s offer. On the cave floor she holds out the sea rose. A takes it, waking every sleeper at once, but it binds you to her. B, or swimming away, refuses.
  • The moon-boat ride follows, played like Act I’s voyage: nothing can hurt you. Sail forward and back, change row, and leap for the pearls that float high.

Act III — The Sea King’s Court

Cross the Sea King’s hall to the treasury pedestal beside the throne and take her pearl. The floor has three grooves, like Act I’s rows; the courtiers glide along them on the beat.

  • Walk left and right; up and down change groove.
  • The Sea King’s lantern sweeps the hall every so often. First it glows and an arrow runs along the floor to show which side it will come from; then its beam crosses the whole stage and leaves by the far side. Caught in the light, you lose a lantern.
  • Hide: press A beside a pink, blue or green courtier on your groove to strike a courtly pose; you stay posed until you move. The beam passes you by, and once it has gone it is gone until the next warning. The seahorse guards give no cover, and walking into one costs a lantern.
  • The red sentries crouch along the front groove and snap their jaws on every beat. Step past between snaps, or use the middle and back grooves. If you took the witch’s rose, they sleep.
  • The whale glides across the hall every 20–25 s and blinds the lantern while it passes. While it passes the lantern can’t see you anywhere. A passing fish blinds the lantern too, for a moment.
  • Her pearl rests on the pedestal at the far right end of the hall. Walk up to it on any groove to take it and end the act.
  • Pearls lie along the grooves; walk over them on their groove.
  • With the full tea reading, an omen glints a beat before the beam or a jaw reaches you.

Act IV — The Whale’s Road

Swallowed whole. Climb across the floating wreckage and the rib ledges of the whale’s belly to the daylight in its throat, then walk out.

  • Walk left and right; A leaps. Hold A for a full leap, or let go early for a short hop.
  • The whale breathes every four bars. The water rises as it breathes in (four cue beats warn you), carrying the barrels, crates and the piano up with it, then drains away. Some ledges can only be reached at high water.
  • The set rocks on the beat. Loose wreckage slides downhill in its slot and carries you with it; you never slip off a piece you’ve landed on. The rib ledges are fixed.
  • Catch the edge. Fall just short of a platform you leapt for and he grabs its edge and hangs, safe from the water. Press toward it, up or A to climb on (he climbs on his own after a moment); press away or down to let go. Walking off an edge on purpose just drops you.
  • A lantern is lost by falling into deep water, by touching the bare stomach floor at low water, or to a swinging fish-hook, which also knocks you back. After a fall you climb back onto the last platform you stood on.
  • The stone lies on a high side ledge above the throat. It is a detour, but in Act V it can break the locked shutter, and it matters at the end.
  • Pearls hang over the platforms and in the gaps between them.

Act V — The Enchanted Tower

Climb the cliff to the stone house at the top. Then, as the witch’s storm sets it alight, climb up through the burning house to her and carry her out across the quay.

  • On the cliff, the holds you can reach glow gold, each with an arrow showing which way to press. Press that way to climb to it. A dotted arc marked A shows where a leap would take you, to a hold out of reach. A press made just before a move ends happens as soon as you arrive; a red cross means there is no hold that way.
  • Dust gathers over a hold just before a stone falls on it: step aside. Cracked holds crumble a moment after you take them, dropping you to the hold below and costing a lantern.
  • The sheltered gap is a safe hold where falling stones only glance off you. Near the top a stone foothold lifts on every other beat; time your step over the lip.
  • In the house, walk the floors and press up at the foot of the stairs or a ladder (down at the head) to change floor. The ceiling shudders and drops dust before a beam or stones fall. A long fall costs a lantern too.
  • The stone. If you carried it from Act IV, press A by the locked shutter on the first floor to throw it: the ivy outside becomes a shortcut to the top floor.
  • Carry her down. Reach her on the top floor and you lift her; the way you came breaks away behind you, so find the new way down. Carrying her you are slower and can’t leap. Out of the front door, cross the quay to the right while the house falls.
  • The ending follows from what you carried: the sea rose binds you to the witch; without it, her pearl lights the lamp in her window; with neither, the window is dark. Everyone you woke in Act II joins the pageant.

A toy theatre in a prologue and six acts, after Georges Méliès’s film Le Royaume des fées (1903): Prince Bel-Azor’s voyage to rescue Princess Azurine from the witch. Each act is staged with a different piece of stage machinery; its card tells you what the A button does.

Prologue — The Betrothal

The court celebrates the betrothal of Prince Bel-Azor and Princess Azurine. Then the uninvited witch curses it, her demons carry Azurine away, and the fairy Aurora arms the prince for the rescue. It leads straight into Act I.

  • The court: walk up to each fairy until your hand reaches her gift and press A to receive it for your bride (the flower, the ring, the crown and the gold), then take Azurine’s hand.
  • The rampart: run after the demons’ chariot of fire along the parapet; A leaps the fire it drops. The fire burns on: each touch costs one of your three lanterns, and with none left the chase starts again.
  • The armoury: once Aurora has broken the witch’s spell, take up your helmet, your shield and the enchanted sword from the floor, then go.
  • B skips a scene.

Act I — The Royal Crossing

Sail the royal ship across the painted sea toward the witch’s tower before her storms wreck it. The stagehands crank three rows of waves; hop between them.

  • Rocks rise through trapdoors where the water churns. Leap over them or change rows.
  • Lightning falls on whichever row the witch’s cloud hangs above. Leaping won’t help: change rows.

  • The tempest. At the end the witch raises her storm: a scene to watch, as the crew light the ship’s lanterns against the dark. Then she strikes the mast and the ship breaks on the rocks.

Act II — The Water Fairy

On the sea floor the Water Fairy breathes life back into the prince. Swim down through her grotto after Aurora’s sinking sword, waking your drowned crew as you go, and catch it on the cave floor.

  • Swim in any direction; you drift slowly down on your own. A kicks: a burst of speed, then a moment to recover.
  • The sword sinks ahead of you, quickening when you are close behind; it can only be caught once it settles on the cave floor.
  • Air runs down as you swim. Hover in the bubbles over a vent to fill up: bubbles stream round you as you breathe in, and the gauge fills. Run out and you lose a lantern.
  • Wake your crew. Swim up to a sleeping crewman and press X to wake him. A conch horn wakes them for you: swim into one to sound it (each sounds once, then turns grey); while it sings, any crewman you pass wakes and floats up. Once you hold the sword it wakes them too. Everyone you wake joins the wedding procession.
  • Clams open for one beat on their own rhythm: while one is open, swim into it or press X beside it for its pearl. Taken, a clam rests shut, then regrows one more pearl that you can watch grow; after that it stays shut and grey.
  • Hazards: eels lunge from holes whose eyes glow first; jellyfish drift and sting; the red dragon patrols the deep cave. Each costs a lantern.
  • The Water Fairy’s blessing. With the sword in hand, swim to her by the wreck on the cave floor; her blessing revives the rest of your crew.
  • The marine procession follows, in her dolphin-drawn conch carriage: nothing can hurt you. Sail forward and back, change row, and leap for the pearls that float high.

Act III — Neptune’s Court

First walk through the lobster palace: hold X (or down) to bow as you pass each lobster guard, who opens his claws for a bowing prince and throws back one who stands up before him; beyond them, leap with A for the pearls hung among the sea flowers. Then on into Neptune’s court. Neptune will grant passage to the tower. Cross his hall to his seal at the far end while the witch hunts for you through her scrying eye. The floor has three grooves; the courtiers glide along them on the beat.

  • Walk left and right; up and down change groove.
  • The witch’s scrying eye sweeps the hall every so often. First it glows and an arrow runs along the floor to show which side it will come from; then its beam crosses the stage. Caught in it, you lose a lantern.
  • Hide: press A beside a pink, blue or green courtier on your groove to strike a courtly pose; you stay posed until you move. The seahorse guards give no cover, and walking into one costs a lantern.
  • The lobster guards crouch along the front groove and snap their claws on every beat. Step past between snaps, or use the other grooves.
  • Neptune’s whale glides across the hall every 20–25 s and blinds the eye while it passes.
  • Neptune’s seal rests on the pedestal at the far right. Walk up to it on any groove: he grants you passage, and the whale takes you aboard.

Act IV — The Whale’s Passage

Neptune’s whale carries you toward the tower. Climb across the floating wreckage and the rib ledges of its belly to the daylight in its throat; it sets you down on a rocky shore, where Aurora points you to a cave in the cliff: the way to the tower.

  • Walk left and right; A leaps. Hold it for a full leap, or let go early for a short hop.
  • The whale breathes every four bars: the water rises (four cue beats warn you), carrying the wreckage up, then drains away.
  • The set rocks on the beat. Loose wreckage slides in its slot and carries you with it; you never slip off a piece you’ve landed on.
  • Catch the edge. Fall just short of a platform you leapt for and he grabs its edge and hangs. Press toward it, up or A to climb on; away or down lets go.
  • A lantern is lost to deep water, the bare stomach floor at low water, or one of the few fish-hooks swinging over the gaps.
  • Aurora’s lamp lies on a high side ledge above the throat: a detour, but its light carries you through the cavern, and in Act VI opens a barred shutter for a shortcut.
  • The cavern. Run through the dark to the far opening onto the night sea. Leap the black pools (falling in costs a lantern and puts you back on firm rock) from floor to ledge. Stalactites shake when you pass beneath, then fall: keep moving. You see only by your own light, or much further by Aurora’s lamp.

Act V — The Enchanted Tower

Climb the coastal cliff, dive for the witch’s tower across the sea gap, swim it while she swoops at you, and climb the tower wall to Azurine’s window.

  • Climbing: the holds you can reach glow gold, each with an arrow showing which way to press. Press that way to climb to it. A dotted arc marked A shows where a leap would take you. A press made just before a move ends happens on arrival; a red cross means there is no hold that way.
  • Dust gathers over a hold just before a stone falls on it: step aside. Cracked holds crumble a moment after you take them.
  • The dive: at the top of the cliff, walk to the edge and press A to dive into the sea gap.
  • The sea gap: swim right for the tower against the current. When the witch’s shadow darkens the water, press A to dive under her swoop. Aurora’s light drives her off before you reach the tower.
  • The tower wall climbs the same way, up to her window at the top.

Act VI — The Burning Tower

Inside, the witch’s demons set the tower alight. Climb to Azurine and carry her down and out before it falls; then end the witch for good.

  • Walk the floors and press up at the foot of the stairs or a ladder (down at the head) to change floor. The ceiling shudders and drops dust before a beam or stones fall. A long fall costs a lantern.
  • Aurora’s lamp. If you carried it from Act IV, press A by the barred shutter on the first floor: its light opens it, and the ivy outside becomes a shortcut to the top.
  • Carry her down. Reach her on the top floor and you lift her; the way you came breaks away, so find the new way down. Carrying her you are slower and can’t leap. Out of the door, cross the clifftop while the tower falls.
  • The witch’s last attack. She rides at you in a barrel on the cliff. Walk into it to push it toward the edge; when it shakes, step back, because it lurches at you and knocks you down. Push it over into the whirlpool.
  • The wedding. Walk Azurine along the street to the castle gate, past the crew you woke, the fairies and the king and queen, catching the falling petals. Then walk together into the light beneath Aurora for the apotheosis.
