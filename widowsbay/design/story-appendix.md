# Widow's Bay — story appendix: optional expansions

Ideas drawn from the expanded world references in
[`references/expanded-world/`](references/expanded-world/README.md). These are
interpreted illustrations of the full 1903 *Le Royaume des fées*, the film the
video is cut from. None of this is needed for the five acts in
[`acts.md`](acts.md). Each idea stands alone, says what it adds and what it
would cost, and can be taken up, changed or dropped.

What the references add beyond the video:

- the royal betrothal court;
- a moonlit rampart and an abduction by a **bat-winged chariot of fire**;
- **red and teal demon attendants** carrying flame;
- a **hooded crone** in dark teal: the film's antagonist, distinct from the
  pink magical woman;
- a fairy who **arms the hero** with helmet, sword and shield;
- a vision of the **witch's forbidden realm**, its crags under a yellow moon;
- an **island tower across a sea moat**;
- a closer look at the **armoury**, the hall glimpsed in the video at about
  0:30.

## A. Story enrichments that fit the main line

**1. The witch's two faces.** The video's woman in pink and the film's
hooded crone become one character with two faces: the glamour, and what is
under it. This keeps the lyric's sea witch alluring and ambiguous, and gives
the story a reveal.

- **Act II:** refusing the rose makes her drop the glamour for a moment.
  The pink figure flickers into the hooded crone, then back.
- **Act III:** in her place beside the Sea King she wears the glamour. The
  courtiers bow to her.
- **Act V:** the figure on the cliff calling the storm onto the house is the
  crone.
- **Witch ending:** the boat carries the sailor away with the beautiful one,
  and the final frame shows only the hood at the tiller.
- **Cost:** a little logic (a flicker between two cut-outs). Art: `witch-hooded`
  (cut-out, standing and casting) and `witch-hooded-cast`.

**2. Her imps.** The film's red and teal demon servants become the witch's
stagehands, pairs of cheeky costumed imps.

- **Act I:** they ride the storm clouds and drop the lightning, which gives
  the bolt warning a face.
- **Act V:** the fire at the house is set by two imps running across the
  breakaway flats with torches. They are hazards with a pattern, and the
  telegraph is their giggle before they appear.
- **Pageant:** they tumble across it.
- **Cost:** small. Art: `imp-red` and `imp-teal`, two poses each (running,
  and leaping with a torch).

**3. Prologue — The Betrothal** (an overture of about 40 s, before Act I).

- **Scene:** the betrothal court of the film, using the video's opening
  revel (0:10–0:20) as the feast. The sailor gives the beloved the pearl on
  its chain before he sails.
- **The theft:** the hooded witch watches from an arch. That night, past the
  moonlit rampart, her fire chariot carries the pearl away. This is why her
  pearl is in the Sea King's treasury in Act III, and why her chain is empty
  in every picture of her.
- **Play:** a short dance at the feast, pressing on the beat. Then the
  chariot crossing the rampart as a non-interactive flourish.
- **Cost:** a medium-sized scene. Art: `betrothal-backcloth`,
  `rampart-backcloth`, `fire-chariot`, plus the existing cast.

**4. The tea-leaf vision.** When Act I's reading is complete (all three
leaves), a three-second vision card plays as the guitar sinks: the witch's
forbidden realm, crags and towers under a yellow moon. It foreshadows
Encore I below.

- **Cost:** tiny. Art: `vision-realm`, one backcloth in a circular vignette.

## B. Game systems

**5. The Fairy's Gifts: something to spend pearls on.** Pearls only score at
the moment. Between acts, the playbill opens an **Intermission** panel where
the benevolent fairy from the film's arming scene offers gifts for pearls:

| Gift | Cost | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Helmet | 30 pearls | +1 lantern for the next act |
| Shield | 40 pearls | blocks the first hit in each act |
| Sword | 60 pearls | cuts kelp, ropes and hooks, opening short alternate routes in Acts II, IV and V |

The fairy is the "good" magical figure, distinct from the witch, if idea 1
is used. It makes collecting pearls matter and gives a gentle difficulty
lever.

- **Cost:** shell work (the shop panel and the act modifiers) and small act
  hooks. Art: `fairy-giver`, `gift-helmet`, `gift-shield`, `gift-sword`.

**6. The programme: a cast album.** A page of the playbill shows a portrait
for each character, creature and set piece as you meet it: the revellers,
the Sea King, the sentries, the whale, the imps. It also counts sleepers
woken (out of 12), lanterns lit and endings seen (out of 3). It encourages
replaying acts and seeing all three endings.

- **Cost:** shell work only; the portraits reuse act art.

## C. Encores: side quests after the main show

Encores unlock on the playbill once the play has been finished at least once.

**7. Encore I — The Tower across the Moat.** The witch's island tower (the
sea-moat tower and the forbidden realm).

- **Unlock:** finishing the play with the full tea reading.
- **Machines:** a rowboat crossing the moat on wave rows (Act I's machinery)
  with the drawbridge timing on the beat, then a climb up the tower's spiral
  flats.
- **Goal:** free the **sea spirits** of Act I's storm, who turn out to be
  the witch's captives, the drowned of other voyages.
- **Reward:** freed spirits join the pageant in white. If the witch's rose
  was taken, freeing them breaks her hold, and the witch ending gains a
  final frame of the sailor rowing home alone at dawn: a fourth, hopeful
  ending.
- **Cost:** a full act. Art: `moat-backcloth`, `tower-cutaway`, `drawbridge`,
  `rowboat`, `spirit-cage`.

**8. Encore II — The Fire Chariot.** A sky chase above the moonlit rampart:
three rows of cloud flats, the way Act I has wave rows.

- **In general:** chase the bat-winged chariot as its imps drop flame,
  collecting stars.
- **After the witch ending:** you drive the chariot yourself as her
  consort, and the imps race you.
- **Cost:** low to medium, since it reuses Act I's row engine with new art.
  Art: `fire-chariot`, `cloud-row` strips, `star-pickup`.

**9. Encore III — The Armoury Revel.** The deck party of Act I, expanded
into the whole song.

- **Stage:** the royal armoury and betrothal hall become a dance floor.
- **Play:** revellers, imps and the band dance through all 3:28 of the
  recording, with patterns of beats to hit and a combo meter. The best score
  shows on the playbill.
- **Why:** it's the one place the whole song plays as a game. It's the
  cheapest encore, built on the party code that already exists and the
  measured beat grid.
- **Cost:** low. Art: `armoury-backcloth`, plus the revellers already
  requested.

## Recommendation

If we expand, these give the most for the effort:

1. **The Fairy's Gifts.** It gives pearls a purpose.
2. **The witch's two faces and her imps.** A small amount of art buys a real
   reveal, and hazards with faces.
3. **The Armoury Revel.** It reuses the party code, and the whole song
   becomes playable.

The prologue and the tower encore are the larger pieces, worth doing once
the five acts are in. Any of these can be added to the image requests:
they'd go in a Part D, generated the same way as Part C.
