    About Ai-Ching

    The I Ching, the Book of Changes, is one of the oldest Chinese classics: sixty-four figures of six lines, each solid (yang) or broken (yin), with texts that have been read for three thousand years as a mirror for decisions.

    Ai-Ching casts a hexagram the traditional way, as six throws of three coins, but the coins are thrown by a generator seeded from your words and today's date. The same question on the same day gives the same answer, as the old rule says: do not ask twice. A new day, or a new question, casts again.

    Your words also steer the reading: what the question is about (love, work, health…) and what kind of answer it wants (a choice, a time, a reason) decide which counsel comes first, how the page is ordered, which words of the sages appear and what it looks for in the day's events on Wikipedia.

    Above each reading, a short AI opening shows that the question was understood and introduces the answer. Chrome's built-in model writes it on your device when it is ready; otherwise the question and the hexagram go to a small Cloudflare Worker that asks Workers AI, and nothing is stored there beyond a day's cache of the answer. You can turn it off on the home page or under any opening. The reading itself never depends on it.

    You can also speak your question with the microphone button. In Chrome, Edge and Safari the browser's own speech recognition writes it down (Chrome and Edge send the sound to their speech service to do so); in other browsers the recording goes to the same Cloudflare Worker, which transcribes it with Whisper on Workers AI and keeps nothing. The words stay in the box for you to check before you cast.

    The hexagram texts are original renderings of the traditional meanings. For the classic translation, each reading links to James Legge's 1882 version.
