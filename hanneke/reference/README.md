# Hanneke de Neve: reference

Photos of Hanneke de Neve's work, gathered as the source for an atmospheric,
colourful experience built on her pieces and her style. They are reference
only: nothing here is published or loaded by a page. Use them in the image
requests, read them for palette and mark-making, and quote individual works
where the experience is built around one.

Hanneke is a Dutch-born artist who grew up on a farm in the Netherlands and
has lived and worked for decades around Trenton, New Jersey (Cranbury, later
Hamilton). She works in fibercollage, quilts and tapestries, painted and
embroidered fabric, oil, acrylic (including acrylic on silk), watercolour,
monotype, pen and ink, and collage. She is represented by The Artful Deposit
Gallery in Bordentown. She is a personal friend of the repository owner, and
the owner has cleared the use of her work here.

[`contact-sheet.jpg`](contact-sheet.jpg) shows every work on one page.

## In her words

From the statement on the [elevenland gallery](https://www.elevenland.com/hanneke/):

> My fibercollages, paintings, watercolors and mixed media are figurative and
> all linked. They depict an imaginary universe with landscapes, people, still
> lifes and dream. What I am trying to do is to show my love for people,
> beauty and life itself. [...] My work is mostly about people and their inner
> feelings and movement.

> Dreams are in my work, and ideas, made-up stories, together with all my
> thoughts about people and their conditions [...] mixed with my day-to-day
> reality. Work creates more work, each new piece calls for another. [...] I
> use, reuse it, put it in collages and drawings -- take a little here, give a
> little there.

Paraphrased from the 2023 Princeton Info profile ("Artist's Dreams See Light of
Day at Princeton Club"): she keeps a dream book and paints from the images in
it; she invents stories that combine real events; her images sit between
realism and abstraction; a picture must above all have good composition and
colour; she starts by setting a horizon and then divides the picture into
compartments, rooms and landscapes. Recurring subjects are faces, cats, birds,
moods, memories of a small Dutch town, and images taken from her
grandchildren's drawings. She paints fabric upstairs at home and keeps oil,
acrylic and printmaking in the basement studio.

## Style notes for the experience

### Colour (her colourful work)

The colour reference is the fibre work, *Viriditas* and *The Golden Lady*.
Much of the elevenland set is photographed on film from the 1990s and early
2000s and reads duller than the pieces are; push saturation back toward
*Viriditas* and the 1stDibs photos, which are recent and true.

- **Deep grounds, hot accents.** Indigo, black and deep teal grounds carry
  red, orange, saffron and pale gold shapes (moons, hearts, suns, faces). The
  moons-and-hearts fibercollage and the black-tree fibercollage are the
  clearest examples.
- **Green and yellow light.** *Viriditas* ("greenness", Hildegard of Bingen's
  word for the life force) is acid yellow-green on deep teal, flecked with red
  and one violet bloom. *The Golden Lady* and the branch painting are
  saffron, butter yellow and lemon against lilac, sky blue and grass green.
- **Patterned borders.** Quilts and tapestries frame their scene in bands of
  batik, paisley, damask, satin and lace, often three or four different
  prints around one picture. A border of mixed fabric is a natural frame for
  a scene or screen.
- **Compartments.** Pictures are split into rooms and panels (the dancers
  quilt, the stacked collage, the blue woman and rooms), each a little scene
  of its own. This suits a world of rooms or vignettes the player moves
  between.
- **Recurring motifs.** Moons (crescent faces with closed eyes, full discs),
  suns, hearts, birds, bare and leafy trees, women's faces with long necks and
  calm or closed eyes, couples, cats, houses of a Dutch small town.
- **Material.** Visible stitching (running stitch, couched thread, machine
  zigzag), frayed edges, netting, metallic and satin sheen, grommets. Shapes
  are cut, not drawn: edges are a little irregular and colours are flat
  within a piece of cloth.

### Line (her drawings, for the UI)

- **One fluid contour.** Figures are a single unbroken black or brown line,
  drawn fast with a fine pen or brush: oval faces, long necks, almond eyes,
  often closed or downcast, a short straight nose joined to the brow. See the
  embrace drawings, the blue woman, the window watercolour and the brown
  brush drawing over *The Golden Lady* and the branch painting.
- **Line over colour, not around it.** The line sits on loose washes and
  scraped paint without filling them; colour spills past it. UI panels can
  be a soft wash with a hand-drawn rule laid over it slightly off register.
- **Handwriting.** Lines of her looping script run across the watercolour
  face and the stacked collage. Handwritten labels or a script face suit
  titles and notes.
- **Pinned and framed.** The boots drawing pins brown-paper cards inside a
  painted red frame; the collages tape scraps and photos together. Menus can
  be cards pinned or stitched onto a ground.
- **Stitch as a rule.** A running stitch or zigzag makes a good divider,
  border or progress line that ties the UI back to the fibre work.
