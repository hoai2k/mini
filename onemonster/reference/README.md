# Reference: the book

Reference material for the look of the game, taken from Mercer Mayer's
*One Monster After Another* (Golden Press, New York, 1974, "A Golden Book";
about 42 pages; reissued by Rainbird Press in 1993 and by Brighter Child /
Gingham Dog Press in 2001). In the story, Sally Ann posts a letter to her best
friend Lucy Jane, and it is stolen from monster to monster before it finally
reaches Lucy Jane's mailbox.

The photos here are copies of pictures that readers posted online (sources
below). They are the book's own art (and, for two of them, art from Mayer's
*Little Monster's Bedtime Book*), still under copyright: **reference only**.
Nothing in this folder is published with the game (`publish.sh` copies only
`game/`), and none of it should be traced into delivered art. The image
requests in [`../design/image-requests.md`](../design/image-requests.md) link
to these files so an image generator can look at them.

## The story

Checked against the book's own text through the Internet Archive's full-text
search of that scan (short quoted snippets), the page photo above, and two
library guides that summarise it: Sharron L. McElmeel's *My Bag of Book
Tricks* (Teacher Ideas Press, 1989) and *Children's Literature Review*
vol. 11 (Gale, 1986), both also on the Internet Archive. In order:

1. **Sally Ann** writes a letter to her best friend **Lucy Jane**, puts it in
   an envelope and puts the envelope into her mailbox.
2. Before the mailman comes, a **Stamp-Collecting Trollusk** creeps up to the
   mailbox, steals the letter and "gabbled away with a smirk on his snerk".
3. Before he can tear off the stamp, a **Letter-Eating Bombanat** flies "out
   of Nowhere" and snaps up the letter, and flies over the **Blue Ocean of
   Bubbley Goo** (spelled "Bubbley" on the page photo).
4. Before it can settle down and eat the letter, a **Bombanat-Munching
   Grumley** reaches up out of the goo and grabs it. Before the Grumley can
   "slither down into the sea for a nice Bombanat lunch", SNAP, something
   happens to him. *What, and how the letter reaches the ship, is not in the
   snippets: unverified.*
5. The letter is on a ship. Nearing land, the ship is struck by a
   **Furious-Floating Ice-Ferg** (SCRUNNCCCHHH!!). As it goes down, the
   **captain** stuffs a note into a bottle, pushes in the letter "for good
   measure", and throws it into the ocean.
6. Before anyone can find the bottle, a **Wild-'n-Windy Typhoonigator** comes
   storming along, sucking up everything in its path: the whole Blue Ocean of
   Bubbley Goo, the bottle too, then blows itself away. By morning it is so
   full "of fishing boats, bottles, and bubbly goo" that it has to let go.
7. Down falls the bottle, KER-PLOP, right on the head of a **Paper-Munching
   Yalapappus** who lives on the edge of Nowhere. The bottle breaks
   (tinkle-plinkle); out fall the Captain's note and the letter. The
   Yalapappus gleefully munches the note, but before he can munch the letter
   the Trollusk, "who just happened to be passing by", snatches it in his
   snerk and runs away. The Yalapappus trundles after him, "shouting and
   loudly grimming".
8. Suddenly, out of the sky swoops the Bombanat again: SNIP. Then, by way of
   a net, everyone is caught, FLIP-FLOP, by the **Bombanat-Collecting
   Grithix**, who "as everybody knows, is a Bombanat collector".
9. The Grithix "did what anybody should do with a lost letter. He mailed
   it!" The Yalapappus, the Trollusk and the Bombanat watch helplessly as he
   drops it into an official-looking mailbox. Before they can steal the
   mailbox, a **mailman** rides up on an official-looking motorcycle: "Stop!
   Unhand that official mailbox!" He takes the letters and rides away, leaving
   the Yalapappus and the Trollusk wailing at the Grithix, who "didn't care a
   snickle" and walks home with the Bombanat firmly under one arm.
10. When Lucy Jane opens her mailbox that afternoon, inside is the letter.
    The Little Book Review says the letter reads "Nothing ever happens around
    here"; *that line is not confirmed from the scan.*

Monsters, in the order they hold the letter: Trollusk, Bombanat, Grumley,
(the captain), Typhoonigator, Yalapappus, Trollusk, Bombanat, Grithix. The
Ice-Ferg never holds the letter; it sinks the ship.

Names, as spelled in the book's text: Stamp-Collecting Trollusk,
Letter-Eating Bombanat, Bombanat-Munching Grumley (the scan's OCR reads
"Crumley"; the library guides print "Grumley"), Furious-Floating Ice-Ferg,
Wild-'n-Windy Typhoonigator, Paper-Munching Yalapappus, Bombanat-Collecting
Grithix. Mayer's own made-up words in the text include "snerk" (the
Trollusk's snout), "grimming" and "a snickle".

Not verified: Grokipedia and some reviews add a "dodo hidden in the
backgrounds"; the red bird at the cover's bottom right may be it. Readers on
Goodreads (average about 4.5) and bloggers remember the book for its funny
names, its busy detailed pages and monsters that are odd rather than scary;
no contemporary professional review was found.

## The style

- **Line.** Dense, wiry pen-and-ink: fur drawn hair by hair, foliage in
  clusters of tiny leaves, crosshatching for every shadow, clouds and waves as
  rows of curling strokes. Line does the modelling; the colour sits on top.
- **Colour.** Thin washes, printed a little faded as in 1970s Golden Books:
  dusty mauve, lilac and pink skies, olive, sage and teal greens, ochre and
  mustard, brick red and red-and-white stripes as accents, slate and
  blue-grey for sea and storm. Pink for the monsters' snouts, hands and feet.
- **The page.** Big painted pictures, full bleed, often across the spread,
  with the text set beneath in a plain white band, in a serif with a large
  ornamental drop capital (the page-20 photo). The cover sets the title in a
  chunky, bouncy yellow display face on a black arch.
- **The monsters.** Large, shaggy and expressive, with pink snouts, tusks or
  fangs, pointed ears, heavy-lidded or bulging eyes and clawed pink paws, and
  a scrap of clothing (a cap, a bib, a necktie, a napkin). They look greedy,
  sly or put-upon, never terrifying.
- **Mood.** Busy, funny and a little grotesque, a chase from page to page,
  ending in a quiet joke. The game follows: slapstick and bustle, nothing
  scary.

## The monsters

What each looks like, as far as the pictures show. Where no picture was
found, it says so; those designs in the image requests are ours and should be
checked against the Internet Archive scan.

- **Stamp-Collecting Trollusk** (cover; Bedtime Book page). A squat, shaggy
  monster with dark-brown fur, a long pink pig-like snout (his "snerk"), a
  tusk, big pointed pink ears and pink hands and feet with claws. On the cover
  he wears a squashed blue-grey cap and a yellow bib lettered "STAMPS"; in the
  Bedtime Book, a battered blue top hat, poring over a stamp album.
- **Letter-Eating Bombanat** (cover; identified by inference, as the flying
  monster that seizes the Trollusk). Huge mauve bat wings with clawed tips, a
  long pink crocodile-like snout full of little fangs, a sly frowning eye, a
  tuft of dark fur on the chest, a red-and-white striped necktie or collar, a
  pink striped cap with a yellow flower on top, and clawed feet.
- **Bombanat-Munching Grumley** (cover, probably). A big shaggy monster in
  ochre-yellow fur, with a wide grinning mouth, two great tusks, a white
  napkin tied at the neck and a fork held up, ready for lunch. That this is
  the Grumley is inferred from the napkin and fork, not confirmed.
- **Wild-'n-Windy Typhoonigator** (page-20 spread). A storm cloud with a
  face: billowing grey-brown and lilac cloud for a body and hair, clawed cloud
  hands, two bulging round eyes under heavy brows, a long pink nose and huge
  pink lips sucking up the sea in a tall spout of streaked blue-grey water.
- **Furious-Floating Ice-Ferg**. No picture found.
- **Paper-Munching Yalapappus**. No picture found. The text gives him paws
  and has him trundle.
- **Bombanat-Collecting Grithix**. No picture found. The text gives him a net.
- **The girls and the mailman.** The girl in the mailbox picture has long
  wavy brown hair, round glasses, a white top and a pink skirt, and is
  barefoot. The mailman (on an official-looking motorcycle) is not pictured
  here.
- **The captain and crew** (page-20 spread). Bearded sailors in grey caps and
  coats, rowing a pink rubber raft with an S.O.S. flag.
