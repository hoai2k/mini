# Reference: the book

Reference material for the look of the game, taken from Arnold Lobel's
*The Ice-Cream Cone Coot and Other Rare Birds* (Parents' Magazine Press,
1971, about 40 pages, long out of print). The book is a set of short rhymes,
one or two lines each, about imaginary birds that are everyday objects.

The photos here are copies of pictures that readers posted online (sources
below). They are the book's own art, still under copyright: **reference
only**. Nothing in this folder is published with the game (`publish.sh`
copies only `game/`), and none of it should be traced into delivered art.
The image requests in [`../design/image-requests.md`](../design/image-requests.md)
link to these files so an image generator can look at them.

## The style

- **Line.** Fine brown-black pen line, wiry and slightly nervous, with
  crosshatching and stippling for shade. Outlines are thin and broken rather
  than bold.
- **Colour.** Transparent watercolour washes, soft and a little faded, as
  printed in 1971: lemon and mustard yellow, olive and sage green, strawberry
  and dusty pink, mauve, ochre, slate blue, warm grey. Colour sits inside the
  ink and often doesn't fill it completely.
- **The page.** Most birds stand alone on a plain cream page, with a short
  rhyme in a clean sans-serif above or below. Only a few have a setting, and it
  is then framed: a round night vignette (the Bobolinks), a tall panel strip
  (the Plume), a plain square of colour (the Piffle). The endpapers are the one
  crowded picture, every bird together on yellow.
- **The birds.** Each is the object first and a bird second: the object keeps
  its real shape and detail, and a bird grows out of it. Shared features: one
  round staring eye with a dot pupil, a long pointed beak, plumes or a few
  curly feathers for crest and tail, and long, thin, knobbly legs with spiky,
  spread claws. They stand very upright and still, as if posing for a field
  guide, and none of them smiles.
- **Mood.** Whimsical, quiet and slightly eerie. Readers remember the book as
  a little creepy, in a way they liked. The game's tone follows: gentle humour
  and odd quiet places, never loud or cartoony.

## The birds

What each bird looks like in the book, as far as the photos show. These notes
are the source for the portrait prompts.

- **Ice-Cream Cone Coot** (cover). A waffle cone lying on its side with a
  lumpy lemon-yellow scoop as its chest. A candy-striped red-and-yellow neck
  leads to a strawberry-scoop head with one big pale-blue eye and a little
  waffle-cone beak. Three orange wafer fingers with magenta bobbles make its
  crest. Pink-and-white striped legs, magenta claws. It stands on a green
  hillock.
- **Cupadee** (cup page). A pale-yellow teacup with a pink band, tipped
  forward. The handle is its tail, with a yellow tuft. A long ochre beak, very
  long thin legs. Its rhyme: another bird tips its empty head and asks you to
  pour tea into the Cupadee without spilling.
- **Shuttercluck** (cup page). A boxy dark camera with a viewfinder and a knob
  on top. The round lens, ringed in yellow, is its eye. A long tan beak, curly
  pink plumes for crest and tail, short orange legs. It never sings or flies,
  but takes your picture with its round eye.
- **Gramophone Gullfinch**. In olive and mustard. A huge fluted horn flares
  behind its small finch head, which has a crest of curved yellow feathers. A
  scaly feathered neck leads to a record-player box with a record, tone arm
  and crank. It stands on one long leg. It sings when you put on a record and
  wind it up.
- **Flowerpot Plume**. A dusty-pink flowerpot with a small face (round eye,
  magenta beak), pink wing and tail, orange legs. A watering-can rose rains on
  it, and the sprout in its pot grows into a towering many-coloured bloom.
  Water it every day.
- **Highbutton Bobolinks**. Two mauve-pink high-button ladies' boots, each
  with a yellow eye and a long white beak pointing up out of the top. They sit
  on a gnarled branch under a small yellow moon and croon to the night, one
  for the left foot and one for the right.
- **Newspaper Peeper**. An upright sheet of newspaper covered in tiny print,
  with a headline (in the book, "BIRD SEED PRICES UP") and two little printed
  photos of birds. Torn strips curl up as its crest, and it has a pale-yellow
  head with a long thin beak and yellow legs. It has words instead of
  feathers.
- **Pincushion Piffle**. A plump olive-ochre cushion bristling with pins:
  pearl-headed pins, hatpins, needles, safety pins. A dimpled thimble sits on
  the front, with a short pointed tail and thin dark legs. It helps with the
  sewing.
- **Dollarbill Dodo**. A banknote held flat like a plank, with curly yellow-
  green tufts at the back, a forked yellow beak at the front and two thin
  legs. Easy to spot, and worth quite a lot.
- **Safecrossing Crow** (endpapers, bottom right). Its head is a dark-green
  cast-iron traffic-signal lantern with red and amber lamps showing, a knob on
  top, small side spikes and an orange tail tuft. It sits on a long black pole
  neck above very long black stilt legs with pink claws.

Other birds named in the book or seen on the endpapers, for later: the
Waterglass Goose, the Milkbottle Midge, the Soupladle Lark, the Wetweather
Wren, the Key Crane, the Jackknife Niffy, the Broomwhisk and a bird called
the Plugsock. The endpapers also show birds made from scissors, a broom, a
dustbin, a tap, a pocket watch, a ladle, a key, a saw, a light bulb, a
rubber glove and a portable radio.
