How to drum

Objective. Lead the sisters’ army across the field and destroy the goal: the straw targets in training, the Han fort in battle. You lose if the army’s strength runs out.

Two drummers. Trưng Trắc plays the deep centre of the bronze drum — tùng. Trưng Nhị plays the rim — cắc.

Commands. Each command is a four-beat rhythm. Movement starts on Trắc’s deep stroke (tùng), attacks on Nhị’s rim stroke (cắc). Play it at any speed you like — what counts is that it is even: every beat the same length. A pair (two dots joined underneath) is two quick strokes sharing one beat; a triplet (marked 3) is three. Play them as quickly as feels natural: what has to be even is the beat itself, so land the stroke after them on its beat. If a stroke breaks the rhythm, the dots show where it fell, crossed out, and you start again; stop for too long and the phrase fades. The first time a new rhythm comes up, the drum plays it for you while a pointer follows the buttons; miss a rhythm three times and it is played again. The dots above the drum show the rhythm you are playing: solid for strokes played, faint for those to come.

Difficulty (in Settings) changes the rhythms. On Easy there are no triplets: plain beats, with pairs for the stronger tiers. Hard adds rests, shown as a short dash: a silent beat, or a silent stroke inside a pair or a triplet. Leave the gap and play the next stroke in its place. Number of beats (also in Settings) sets how long every command and call is, four by default: the rhythms keep their first and last beats and what makes each tier different, and grow or shrink in the middle. Stronger rhythms. Every command has three tiers. Tier I is four plain strokes; tiers II and III add pairs and triplets and hit harder (×1.4 and ×1.96). Evenness adds up to a further ×1.4 on top: a sloppy tier II still beats a perfect tier I, and the more even you play, the more it hits.

The army answers. When a rhythm is accepted, the army acts for four beats at your speed while the drum crew plays your rhythm once more, its dots lighting with each stroke; your dots stay lit until the drum glows, ready for the next command. The army moves on your strokes, at your speed. A march or a strike carries on in your rhythm for up to two more rounds (weaker) until you start the next command. Nhị’s flourish: while the army answers, rim strokes placed between its beats add power (up to four). Other strokes during the answer are simply ignored.

Shield. Defence is separate from the drum. Han crossbow bolts fly in a visible arc, and a spearman draws back before he lunges. Press shield while an attack is on its way and the army throws a shield wall over the whole group: everything already in the air is deflected (“Defended”). It is ready again almost at once. The same button clears the rhythm dots: raising the shields starts your rhythm afresh, so shield, then drum the next command. In chapters without shields, A (Space) just clears the dots.

The drum wakes. Start the next command within two beats of the drum glowing ready to chain. Four in a row and the drum wakes: every command is half again as strong.

Phones and tablets. Hold the device sideways. The big drum at the bottom is played like the real one: tap its centre for Trắc’s tùng and the red ring round its rim for Nhị’s cắc. On a phone you can also drum with your thumbs: anywhere on the left side of the screen is tùng, the right side cắc. Use several fingers at once for quick pairs and triplets. Tap the battle itself to raise the shields, and swipe across the drum or the dots to reset.

Drum Practice

From the title screen, Drum Practice has no right or wrong, only the drum. Rhythms are counted in beats (four, like a command, unless you change it), and each beat may hold one, two or three strokes, or none. In free drumming, drum any rhythm of that many beats at your own speed. It shows as faint dots while you play and settles into the finished rhythm when its last beat ends. The drum crew then plays it back again and again while dancers gather at the fire. Drum along with it as long as you like: it keeps playing until you clear it with A (Space, or a swipe), and the next rhythm you drum takes its place. In call and answer, a partner drums a call and you answer with any rhythm of the same length. The call and your answer then play together, again and again, and you can drum along. Clear it with A and it is your turn to call: your partner answers with a riff on your rhythm, and that plays on until you clear it. Each clear hands the call to the other side.

Rests are allowed: leave a beat, or a stroke inside a beat, silent. Strokes are read as plain beats and pairs unless they plainly fall in threes. An answer is read at about the call’s speed. A stray stroke or two before a rhythm, or extra strokes after its last beat, are left out. Beside the bronze drum sits a secondary drum, struck with Y: its strokes are part of the rhythm and the crew plays them back on it.

Training lists every rhythm the chapters ask for, numbered and sorted into five levels, from steady beats to triplet runs; each row says where the chapters use it. Choose a level and a rhythm: your partner plays it and you play it back, judged as in the game, and each rhythm played well leads on to the next. The rhythms are shown as designed (Medium, four beats), whatever the Difficulty and Number of beats settings.
