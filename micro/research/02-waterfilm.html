Scale band 02 · micro-animals

The water-film world.

Tardigrades and rotifers make hydration, attachment and particle transport visible at the boundary between terrain and fluid.

Field notes / 30 October 2026 · Biology, visual references & possibilities for simulation

Representative body size
Tardigrades commonly ~250–500 µm
Useful observing range
40–100× total; 200–400× for detail
Illustrative field width
4.5–1.8 mm at FN18 / 4–10× objectives

A thin wet habitat, not a tiny ocean.

Tardigrades have four pairs of unjointed lobopod legs with claws. Many active moss dwellers need a water film; their dry inactive state is biologically different. Feeding varies among species, including grazing and predation. A typical adult size is only a guide: the wider reported range is much larger. [S02]

Rotifers add a different silhouette: an extensible body, an anchoring foot in many forms, and a ciliated feeding crown. A crown creates transport of small particles; it is not a pair of rotating saw blades. The selected Philodina reference is a bdelloid rotifer; other rotifers can feed or move differently. [S23]

The museum’s dormancy account is a useful antidote to the “indestructible water bear” trope. Survival under an experimental condition does not imply an active animal can behave normally there. [S03]

2D and 3D reveal different constraints.

2D possibility

The wet mount

A mostly planar brightfield or DIC view can reveal feet, gut contents and leaf cells. Water-film thickness can vary across the surface; a narrowing connection can isolate a patch without turning the world into a side-scrolling aquarium.

A coverslip is a real constraint if this is a laboratory sample: compression and reduced vertical freedom should be acknowledged.

3D possibility

Between a leaf and an interface

Use leaf ridges and film thickness to create a shallow but real volume. A 300 µm body cannot occupy a water gap much thinner than its own thickness without deformation or exposure.

Menisci can define the boundary. They should not be a collection of giant free-standing bubbles. Immersive camera lighting is an interpretation, not what a light microscope directly records.

Concept board / water-film world.

What is still uncertain?

Cryptobiosis tolerance, preparation time, recovery and feeding preference are species-dependent. The SEM reference represents an animal prepared from an active state, not a living animal walking in a high-vacuum microscope. Use the real micrograph for cuticle and claw reference; use filmed living specimens for gait. Compare electron imaging before adopting its look.
