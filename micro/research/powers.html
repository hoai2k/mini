Gameplay research · affordances & constraints

Powers already exist.

Biology offers shape change, anchoring, sensing, feeding and dormancy. Their limitations may be as interesting as their effects.

Field notes / 30 October 2026 · Biology, visual references & possibilities for simulation

Possibility space, not a game design

The entries below are interaction hypotheses. They do not select a protagonist, prescribe goals, define a progression system, balance statistics or assemble a game loop. Every “power” should retain a cost, a physical requirement and an honest distinction between evidence and invention.

Dynamics that could emerge from combinations.

Attachment versus access

A fixed feeder has a stable position but a limited feeding reach. A mobile cell can find other patches but must keep propelling or maintaining contact. This could make location meaningful without territorial behavior being assumed.

Spatial tradeoff

Changing shape versus moving fast

An amoeba’s body can wrap a surface while a ciliate moves around it. The contrast suggests different responses to the same gap, rather than a single movement controller with different speed values.

Movement contrast

Wet connection versus dormant refuge

A receding film could disconnect food patches. Remaining active, finding a wetter recess and entering an inactive state imply distinct outcomes; none should be treated as a universally superior action.

Habitat change

Feeding creates transport

Suspended particles can reveal a local current, and an attached organism can change what moves nearby. Neighbor interactions could follow from local transport without granting control of the entire fluid.

Local environmental coupling

Time is another scale to keep honest.

A ciliary avoidance response, slow amoeboid reshaping, digestion, reproduction and survival through drying do not share one natural clock. A visualization can compress time, but should say which processes are accelerated and by how much. A cell dividing during an encounter is not a neutral substitute for a much slower population event.

Likewise, fluid drag, contact and response times do not become familiar animal inertia just because the camera is immersive. At low Reynolds number, sustained motion needs sustained propulsion, while ambient flow can still transport the organism. [S15]

Avoid attractive but misleading powers.

  • Universal absorption: edible material, dissolved nutrients and large prey involve different uptake mechanisms.
  • Magic light: fluorescence needs excitation and an emitting structure; chemical maps are explanatory data.
  • Instant evolution: different species’ traits are not interchangeable upgrades on demand.
  • Perfect radar: sensing can be local, noisy and modality-specific; an eyespot is not a camera.
  • Tardigrade immunity: resistance depends on state, species and exposure conditions.
  • Generic sticky water: quantify the scale and speed; include walls, interfaces and substrate attachment when relevant.

Questions that preserve room for invention.

Which trait is visually readable at the chosen field size? What physical substrate or resource does it require? What happens when that requirement disappears? Does it create a different relationship with the environment, or merely a bigger number? Would the same interaction read differently in a 2D optical view and a 3D immersive one?

Those questions can guide later experiments while leaving the actual game undesigned.
