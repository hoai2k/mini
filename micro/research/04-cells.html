Small cells, large surroundings.

Euglena gracilis is a useful anchor: the cited microaquarium study gives typical lengths of 35–50 µm. Other euglenids can be substantially larger. Here a 45 µm cell moves past a surface film, with selected tens-of-micrometres diatoms as rigid geometric neighbors. [S12]

Phototaxis in E. gracilis depends on its light-sensing apparatus and illumination conditions. Its red stigma works with a photoreceptor; it is not an image-forming eye. Responses can favor or avoid light depending on intensity. This suggests sensing and orientation, rather than a permanent “go toward the brightest thing” behavior. [S13]

Reference context matters

The diatom photograph is from Antarctic marine sea ice, and the biofilm SEM is from marine seagrass. They are useful morphology and texture analogues. They are not documentation of the same freshwater community as Euglena or the moss animals.

How to make both 2D and 3D informative.

2D possibility

A field of cells and trajectories

At 400× total magnification, a 45 µm cell occupies about one tenth of a 450 µm field. Phase contrast can emphasize transparent cells, with halos identified as imaging artifacts. A fixed surface layer is natural for exploring diatom traction and biofilm contact.

Bacteria should remain small dots or rods; do not give each a legible fantasy face. Brownian motion is more important for small particles than for an entire large protist.

3D possibility

A surface with a shallow sky of water

Use a few cell lengths of volume above a matrix-coated surface. Rigid silica shells, softer cells and sparse debris give material contrast. Avoid a limitless ocean with a tiny reef rescaled into it.

Scattering, refraction and biological pigments could guide a physically motivated rendering, but optical diffraction and camera resolution still matter. A hypothetical microscopic camera is not a camera that could literally fit beside these cells.

Concept board / small-cell neighborhood.

Where this collection stops.

A closer study could enter ultrastructure, organelles and molecular processes, but that is a different environment and time scale. Ordinary light microscopy cannot resolve a lipid bilayer as a pair of distinct physical surfaces. TEM sections reveal internal structure through preparation, not a navigable miniature room inside a living cell. [S17] · [S22]

Compare visual styles before combining an SEM surface, a TEM interior and living brightfield motion in the same image.
