How to play

Each picture is one of Hanneke de Neve's works, rebuilt as a live patchwork: a ground, with cut pieces of cloth laid on it. Reach in and move it.

Every stick, trigger and button is tied to something in the picture. Some do small things: a moon slides, a face tilts, a heart swells. A few do big things: the whole picture swirls, a tide runs through it, the next picture pours in. Nothing tells you which is which, so try them. Once you have found a control, the piece it moves stays outlined in your thread colour.

Small moves set up the big ones. A big control is centred on one piece, and how far it reaches depends on the picture: bring that piece to the middle, sew other pieces to it, or bring it to the piece it belongs with. A dotted ring round it shows its reach and tightens as it gets ready. When it wakes, lean on it: big effects can carry every piece somewhere new, or carry you into the next picture. Then there are new controls to find.

There is nothing to win or lose. Leave the picture alone and the pieces drift home. It is never quite still: the wind bends the trees and blows leaves across, and the people breathe.

Up to four players. Each controller is a player with its own thread colour and its own controls; the keyboard and mouse are one more. One player can set up what another sets off.
